Introduction to designing blended learning

Blended learning

Modes of work: synchronous and asynchronous

Examples of teaching and learning methods in each mode.

Synchronous mode

Real-time interaction

Live lecture / presentation

Classes delivered face-to-face or online in real time.

Discussion / debate

Live exchange of views and arguments with the academic teacher and participants.

Group work (breakout rooms / workshops)

Collaboration in small groups during face-to-face or online classes.

Q&A session

Asking questions directly and getting answers in real time.

Live polls / surveys

Quickly gathering opinions and feedback during classes.

Live case analysis

Jointly analysing a problem situation and drawing conclusions in real time.

Asynchronous mode

Self-paced learning

Materials for independent study

Reading texts, articles, e-books, presentations or other materials.

Video recordings / podcasts

Watching or listening to learning content at any time.

Individual assignments

Completing exercises, quizzes and tests at a convenient time.

Discussion forums

Taking part in discussions and exchanging opinions at a convenient time.

Projects / long-term assignments

Working on projects carried out in stages, according to one's own schedule.

Reflection journal / portfolio

Keeping a learning journal and reflecting on progress and experiences.

Key to success

combine both modes deliberately — synchronous where interaction and collaboration are needed; asynchronous where individual pace and deepening knowledge matter. Together, they create a coherent learning experience.

Academic course design — 1/2

Seven steps for designing a course

Good classes do not happen by chance — they are designed with students, their needs and the intended outcomes in mind.

01

Define learning objectives and outcomes

Determine what students should know, understand and be able to do after the class.

02

Analyse the context

Get to know the group's needs, level of preparation and expectations, as well as the conditions for delivering the class.

03

Design learning activities

Select methods and forms of work that suit the objectives and foster active learning.

04

Select methods, materials and tools

Choose methods, materials and tools (including digital ones) that support the delivery of the class.

05

Plan assessment

Design methods of assessing learning outcomes and assessment criteria.

06

Plan the session structure

Determine the structure of the session, time allocation and the sequence of learning activities.

07

Deliver and monitor

Monitor the session flow, respond to needs and support students.

Syllabus — point of reference

Before you start completing the syllabus

First, review the study programme of the field of study in which the classes will be taught.

01

Identify the correct programme

Check the field and mode of study, the academic year of the syllabus and the semester in which the course is delivered. The programme is selected according to the year in which the group started their studies.

02

Establish where the course fits

The study programme shows where the course fits in the educational process, its intended outcomes and the assigned workload.

03

Ensure continuity

Check what content the group has already covered. Avoid repeating topics and take prior knowledge and skills into account.

Good practices — syllabus

How to prepare a useful and coherent syllabus?

Seven principles that support the transparency, coherence and quality of teaching documentation.

Good practices for completing syllabuses

Four principles on language, coherence, the readers' perspective and a realistic workload. Transparent rules, regular updates and alignment of the document with the actual delivery of the course.

01

Use inclusive language

Use neutral wording and keep the same terminology throughout the syllabus.

1. Use inclusive language — Use neutral wording and keep terminology consistent.

GOOD

Students prepare a project and present it to the academic teacher.

BAD

A student should prepare his project and present it to the lecturer.

02

Design the syllabus as a whole

Objectives, outcomes, content, methods and assessment should form a coherent system.

GOOD

Outcome: “analyses data”; classes: case studies; assessment: analytical task.

BAD

The outcome concerns analysis, the classes are lecture-only, and the pass requirement is a recall test.

03

Write from the students' perspective

State clearly what students will learn and what they will do.

GOOD

In class, students learn about management methods and how to match them to different situations.

BAD

Implementation of topics covering issues of contemporary management methods.

04

Set realistic requirements

The workload should correspond to the ECTS credits and the actual time needed to complete tasks.

4. Set realistic requirements — The workload should correspond to the ECTS credits and the actual time required for tasks.

GOOD

Team project: 15 hours, including analysis, developing a solution and a presentation.

BAD

Reading 500 pages, conducting research and writing a report: 10 hours.

05

Treat the syllabus as an “educational contract”

Specify the requirements, assessment, pass requirements and the permitted scope of AI use.

5. Treat the syllabus as an “educational contract” — Specify the requirements, assessment, pass requirements and the scope of AI use.

GOOD

Project 40%, presentation 20%, exam 40%; any use of AI must be disclosed.

BAD

The grade depends on participation and passing; AI use guidelines will be provided later.

06

Update the syllabus before each edition

Review the reading list, methods, independent study, assessment, digital tools and AI use guidelines.

6. Update the syllabus before each edition — Review the reading list, methods, independent study, assessment, digital tools and AI.

GOOD

Before the new edition, the reading list, project, workload and AI use guidelines were updated.

BAD

The syllabus was copied without review despite changes to the reading list, assignments and pass requirements.

07

Treat the syllabus as a quality tool

Document how the course is actually delivered and its alignment with the study programme.

7. Ensure the syllabus is aligned with how the course is delivered — The document should reflect the study programme, the actual learning activities and the assessment methods.

GOOD

The outcomes derive from the study programme, the content develops them and the assignment makes it possible to assess them.

BAD

The syllabus specifies a test, although what is actually assessed is a project not described in the document.

Key principle

The syllabus should describe a real, coherent and achievable learning process, not merely meet formal requirements.

Good practices — 3/3

Update, communicate and ensure alignment

Precise rules reduce misunderstandings, and an up-to-date syllabus reflects the actual delivery of the course.

01

Treat it as an “educational contract”

Specify the requirements, assessment rules, expectations and AI use guidelines precisely. Students should know on what basis they will be assessed.

02

Update before each edition

Check that the reading list is up to date, and review teaching methods, workload, assignments, methods of assessing learning outcomes and information on digital tools and AI.

03

Ensure alignment with delivery

The syllabus should reflect the study programme and the actual delivery of the course. It serves as a point of reference in educational quality reviews and accreditation processes.
